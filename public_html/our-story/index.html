The authentic 2016 origin story

We were delivering in Kerala before the giants even landed.

Back in 2016–2017, Fooody pioneered app-based delivery in Kerala. We were in the trenches—personally painting our office walls, running custom Facebook ordering bots, printing t-shirts, and convincing restaurants to go digital.

Swiggy entered Kochi on April 3, 2018. Zomato expanded across Kerala cities like Trivandrum, Thrissur, and Alappuzha on June 27, 2018.

Fooody paused, learned, and now returns in 2026 to solve the real crisis facing Indian food businesses: margin-killing aggregator commissions and total lack of customer ownership.

Founder & freedom manifesto

Reclaim the restaurant-first ecosystem.

I started Fooody in Kerala when “order food on an app” still sounded like a dare. We painted walls, printed tees, wrote Messenger bots by hand, and asked restaurants to trust a future they could not yet see.

The giants arrived with capital. We paused. We watched what scale without ownership does to a kitchen: thinner margins, rented customers, and brands that no longer know who ate last night.

Getting orders is no longer the challenge. Owning those orders is. What gets measured gets managed, and what gets owned gets scaled. In 2026, Fooody returns to give restaurants across India a direct channel they actually own — starting from the state that taught us the work.

— Athul Anil

Why this history matters

The giants taught Kerala to order. They never taught kitchens to own.

Athul Anil’s 2016 startup was not a pitch-deck romance. It was wet paint on office walls, late-night Facebook bot code, and first-generation merch handed to riders who still had to explain what “order on an app” meant.

When national capital arrived in 2018, Fooody paused. The 2026 return is the unfinished sentence: restaurants across India deserve a direct store at fooody.in/their-brand — with 0% commission and 100% of their guests.
